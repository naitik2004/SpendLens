# GTM.md

## Exact target user

**Primary:** Engineering manager or CTO at a 5–25 person seed/Series A startup. They approved the AI tool budget 6–12 months ago, it auto-renews, and they haven't looked at it since. They're not cheap — they're busy. The bill comes out of the company card and gets mentally filed under "necessary."

**Secondary:** Indie founder or solo developer who pays for 2-3 tools and has a vague sense they're overlapping but hasn't done the math.

Job title: Engineering Manager, VP Engineering, CTO, or Founding Engineer. Company stage: post-product, pre-profitability, 5–50 people.

## First 100 users in 30 days, $0 budget

**Week 1 — Seeding with real value**
Post a genuine analysis tweet: "I audited our AI tool spend and found we were paying $40/seat for Cursor Business when $20/seat Pro does the same thing for teams under 5. Here's how to check your own stack: [link]" — no product pitch, just the insight. Tag it with actual numbers.

**Week 2 — HN Show HN post**
"Show HN: SpendLens — free AI spend audit for engineering teams." The title must promise and deliver something specific. Post at 8am PT Tuesday. Reply to every comment within the first 2 hours. This alone can drive 300-500 visitors if the tool is genuinely useful.

**Week 3 — Targeted cold DMs on X**
Search for tweets mentioning "cursor subscription" or "github copilot team" or "chatgpt enterprise price." These people are already thinking about the cost. DM: "Saw your tweet about Cursor pricing — built a free tool that audits your whole AI stack in 2 minutes, would love your feedback." 50 DMs, expect 5-10 replies.

**Week 4 — Indie Hackers and relevant Slack groups**
Post in IH with the actual numbers from real audits (anonymised). "We've run 50 audits — the most common waste is paying for Cursor Business at $40/seat when Pro covers the same workflow at $20." Specifics beat vague pitches.
